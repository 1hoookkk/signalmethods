# Workstation decisions

## LPC envelope and corner peaks

- ENVELOPE follows the preserved Spectrogram README's stated sequence: order-12 LPC, synthesis-filter impulse response, then FFT magnitude; an authored bell-filter response is not the input envelope.
- Resample speech to 11,025 Hz with MATLAB's antialiasing FIR before LPC; sources below that rate retain their rate, and playback is not resampled by this analysis operation.
- Retain the specification's 60 ms cursor window and selected analysis window; the README does not specify its LPC preprocessing or impulse-response length, so those details are implementation choices, not verified binary equivalence to the SGI program.
- Remove the slice mean, use MATLAB `lpc` with order 12 and prediction-error gain, and FFT an impulse response of FFT SIZE samples; retain measured amplitude rather than normalizing each envelope to its tallest peak.
- If a deterministic tone makes the order-12 covariance solution singular or unstable, retry its Yule-Walker solution with a 1e-10 relative diagonal regularizer; a valid audible tone must not silently become an empty envelope.
- Read the visible envelope maxima near the LPC conjugate poles; estimate bandwidth from the matching pole radius, then order peaks from low to high frequency so vowel formants feed corresponding SOS rows.
- Keep the full LPC envelope and up to six peak estimates; the five strongest peaks supply the five editable resonant rows, in frequency order, because law 5 reserves row 6 for CEILING.
- Peak measurements are source data; the corner chord remains the editable truth, with the specification's 0.25-semitone pole width and 4-semitone zero width applied before fitting and compilation.
- ENVELOPE selects SPEECH; selecting BELLS leaves ENVELOPE, so the displayed analysis and the reader cannot silently refer to different models.
- Show the analysis only up to its actual Nyquist frequency and place the 3D peak markers at the envelope's height; do not extend low-rate LPC results to 20 kHz.
- Silence clears the peaks and disables a new read through an empty slice chord, so a previous vowel cannot be saved under a silent cursor position.
- The user's resampling request concerns LPC analysis of sound, not sampling the MORPH x Q corner space; the mistakenly added grid operation was removed.

Sources: [preserved Spectrogram README](../../evidence/research-results/emu-sgi-1993/spectrogram/extracted/README), [MATLAB lpc](https://www.mathworks.com/help/signal/ref/lpc.html), [MATLAB resample](https://www.mathworks.com/help/signal/ref/resample.html).

The full workstation acceptance and remaining build decisions are still being completed; this record does not assert that the whole application has passed acceptance.

## 2026-09-05, HEADSPACE

- Tyson, after his first hour: "make the whole app do one thing. move through the klatt vowels and hrtf notches. bring in some of the dvtd. call it headspace. ctrl s is to save to current corner", then "perform the logarithmic lerp", "the key is capturing in-between and slight nudges not just smooth playback", "fitting is correct but it is a separate workflow".
- HEADSPACE is one screen. A strip of squares, one per frame, sorted low to high by root: the 12 Klatt 1980 vowels, the 15 DVTD subject-1 vowels, the 47 head frames. A gold mark is the position; drag the strip, or arrows step an anchor, Shift+arrows nudge MORPH by 5, Ctrl+arrows by 1. Between two anchors the sound is the log lerp of the voice-led pair: notes linear in semitones, widths geometric, gains linear in dB, compiled at every position. The chip's word lerp is not used here; it is what the plugin plays between the saved corners. The centre plot is what you hear, the two small plots are the anchors either side.
- Four corners along the bottom, M0 Q0 to M1 Q1, the current one framed orange; keys 1 to 4 or a click choose it; Ctrl+S saves the live chord into it, named `a > b 40` for an in-between, and writes the four to `banks/HEADSPACE.bank.json`, which loads on the next launch. WRITE BODY FILE writes the 240 bytes through `bodyCorners` when all four are filled. PLAY, SAW, PINK NOISE. Nothing else.

Cause: `chordToStages` wrote `[]` for an off voice, and the C++ chord loader takes `[]` as an object, so the voice came on at the loader defaults, note 60 and width 1; row 6 then had a live pole with its zero at the ceiling and nothing cancelled it. Fixed on both sides: `chordToStages` writes `null` (NaN through jsonencode), `Library::loadChords` accepts a voice only when it is an object. `compile` also now writes a voice the chord says off at the origin when its raw words hold a conjugate pair, so switching a voice off in a decompiled chord silences it; real and degenerate raw voices (P2K rows) are kept, and the P2K round trip stays within a quarter dB. - Tyson: "It should be more of a triangular shape. Sorted by gain and pitch", then "Think high order geometry". The strip is a field: ROOT across (the frame's lowest pole as a note), dB up (the peak of its unity-DC response); every frame a square where it sits; the gold mark anywhere in it. The sound is the barycentric log blend of the three frames of the Delaunay triangle around the mark (weights sum to one; notes linear in semitones, widths geometric, gains linear; the two lighter frames voice-led to the heaviest, missing voices filled as cancelling pairs), so an edge is the pair lerp and a square is that frame exactly. Outside the hull the mark snaps to the nearest square. The pair was the 1-simplex, the triangle is the 2-simplex of the same blend in chord space; the four-corner body the plugin plays is the next order. Coincident squares are spread by 0.01 semitone so the triangulation stays valid; the triangulation is built on the field normalised to its axis ranges. Arrows step to the next square by ROOT (left, right) or by dB (up, down); Shift nudges 1 semitone or 1 dB, Ctrl 0.2. The three frames around the mark are drawn small beside the live response with their shares.
