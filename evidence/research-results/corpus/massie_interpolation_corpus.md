# Massie on parametric bands and interpolation, 1993 to 2017

Ingested 2026-09-02. Three primary sources plus Rossum Electro-Music forum posts.

## 1. Massie, "An Engineering Study of the Four-Multiply Normalized Ladder Filter"

JAES vol 41 no 7/8, pp 564-582, July 1993. Affiliation E-mu Systems, Scotts Valley.
https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=6994 (paywalled; abstract only)

Abstract, verbatim: "An engineering example of an orthogonal second-order digital
all-pass filter implementation is presented in a fully parametric audio equalizer
application. Independent coefficients control frequency, Q, and boost/cut. The noise
and scaling properties of the four-multiply normalized ladder structure are analyzed
and shown graphically."

Reading: in the Morpheus year, E-mu's DSP lead publishes a second-order parametric
band whose three controls are frequency, Q, boost/cut, each on its own coefficient.

## 3. saol-users 0179, Dana Massie, 1999-11-23

https://sound.media.mit.edu/resources/mpeg4/saol-users/0179.html

- "First, the parametric eq, when implemented with a 5-multiply direct form biquad,
  interpolates perfectly."
- "You apparently only need 8 basis filters to generate all intermediate parametric
  eq filters."
- "Note that a 4 multiply bi quad does *NOT* interpolate properly."
- "I tried looking empirically at what happens to the poles and zeros of filters whose
  parameters are being interpolated, and they follow arcs on the z-plane."
- "In general, outside of the lattice/ladder filters, I would suggest using cascaded
  second order sections, whether they are direct form or otherwise, for interpolation."
- "Another filter type that interpolates very badly is the parallel form."
- "I would not advise using a higher order direct form filter."
- "If you start with a coefficient set that is stable, and you end with a coefficient
  set that is stable, all linear interpolated values in between are also stable."
  (Direct Form II biquad, Moorer's proof, Jackson's "triangle of stability".)
- "For various ladder filters, interpolated filter coefficients can also be shown to be
  stable, as long as the coefficients are on the unit interval."
- "Do the interpolated filters still satisfy the desired design constraints of the
  initial filter prototypes (or 'basis' filters - a nice concept)."
- "The goal is to design a filter structure that satisfies some design constraint for
  all values of the parameters under interpolation."
- "The only certain way to avoid zipper noise is to ramp the coefficients at the sample
  rate." "The same goes for amplitude multipliers - actually, they are even more
  important to ramp."
- "the technique does not work 'properly' with FIR filters."

## What the three together say about the authoring tool

1. The unit is a second-order parametric band with frequency, Q and boost/cut on
   independent coefficients (1993). Six of them in cascade plus a low-pass section
   (Rossum, US10514883B2). Cascade, never parallel (1999).
2. Corners are the only thing authored. Everything between corners is linear
   interpolation of encoded coefficients, and it is legal by construction because a
   parametric band interpolates into a parametric band (1999). "8 basis filters" =
   the cube; the P2K four corners are the square case.
3. Safety is an authoring-time job done by hand per body, not a runtime check (Massie
   1995 on the 24 dB peak; Alpert 2017 "tremendous amount of effort configuring"). In
   the P2K bytes that job is the per-corner trim and the 6 dB step cuts: CUT last.
4. Poles and zeros travel on arcs of the z-plane between corners (1999). The display
   the designer needed is the response at a corner, and the corner-to-corner sweep
   auditioned; not a pole-zero editor.
5. Level at the peak of a resonance is what the designer watched, since the failure
   mode named in every source is a peak stacking on a peak.

## 5. Bristow-Johnson, "The Equivalence of Various Methods of Computing Biquad Coefficients for Audio Parametric Equalizers"

Wave Mechanics, Burlington VT, AES paper (mid-1990s; cites Massie's JAES paper as [7],
misdated 1986 in the reference list; the JAES issue is July 1993).
https://www.collinsaudio.com/Prosound_Workshop/EQ_Coefficients_bristow_johnson.pdf
Acknowledgments: "Dana Massie, Andy Moorer, and Eric Richardson."

- Massie's section, verbatim: "The method of Regalia & Mitra [6] and Massie [7] actually
  doesn't use the direct biquadratic form used above but instead uses an allpass filter
  (implemented as a two stage 4 multiply normalized ladder form) summed to a feedforward
  path", H(z) = 1/2[(1+K) + (1-K)A(z)], "there are only two coefficients to determine".
- The two allpass coefficients map one-to-one onto the user parameters:
  k1 = -cos(w0) (frequency alone), k2 = a2 = pole radius squared term (bandwidth alone),
  K = boost/cut (gain alone). Three independent controls, one coefficient each.
- Bandwidth in Massie's scheme, verbatim: "define bandwidth to be the frequency
  difference between the -3 dB points for a notch filter of -inf dB notch gain. For other
  gains, the distance from zero of the pole pair remains unchanged for constant
  bandwidth." So Q sets the pole radius, independent of gain; gain moves the zero.
- Conclusion: every method yields the same five biquad coefficients given one bandwidth
  definition; the topology is a noise/limit-cycle choice, not a design one.

Reading for the tool: Massie's parametric IS the P2K row. FREQ = the angle word (k1 =
-cos w0), Q = the pole radius word (k2), GAIN = where the zero sits relative to the pole
(K). The verified piano rule, peak = 20 log10(zero bw / pole bw), is this section with K
expressed as a zero radius. The hardware stores the same three things per section as
separate words and interpolates them separately, which is why the lerp stays legal.
