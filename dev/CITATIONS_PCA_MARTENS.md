## The data matrix, and spectral vs temporal PCA

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 35, §3:**
> "To make the distinction between these two methods clear, we must examine the
> orientation of the matrix input to PCA: If the downsampled PVA data matrix is
> organized with rows corresponding to frequency values, and columns
> corresponding to time values, then we have spectral PCA. This analysis
> capitalizes on correlations between the columns of spectral envelopes. If the
> PVA data matrix is transposed, we have temporal PCA that capitalizes on
> correlations between the columns of temporal envelopes."

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 36, §4:**
> "We have described a mechanism for packaging tones of unequal duration into
> downsampled PVA data matrices of 22 frequency values by 200 time values."

**Martens 1987, p. 277, §3:**
> "The result was used to construct a data matrix of 36 azimuth angles (rows) by
> 24 spectral bands (columns) with its rows exhibiting mean of zero and standard
> deviation of one."

## The correspondence problem, and how they solve it

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 35, §2:**
> "One of the major advantages of VDTP is that it maps tones of unequal duration
> into a common-resolution non-linear time domain. In effect, we have overcome
> the obstacle of finding the constant number of temporal variates required for
> analyzing multiple tones in a single PCA, making possible a novel application
> of PCA to PVA datasets."

## Prototype construction — the MANOVA decomposition

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 37, §4:**
> "Now we show the advantage of breaking that spectral PCA into two parts,
> employing methods from Multivariate Analysis of Variance (MANOVA; see Harris,
> 1985). The first part is the generation of a prototypical timbre from a set of
> timbres using a PCA that finds weighting functions only for what the set of
> timbres has in common. The second part is the interpolation between those
> timbres using a PCA that finds weighting functions only for what distinguishes
> between the timbres. The prototype weighting functions were generated by
> finding the eigenvectors of the pooled within-groups sums of squares and
> crossproducts (SSCP) matrix. The interpolation weighting functions were based
> upon the between-groups SSCP matrix. The within-group and between-group SSCP
> matrices sum to equal the total SSCP matrix for all the PVA datasets
> submitted, hence the information for complete reconstruction of the original
> datasets is not lost in these operations."

## Is a mean a valid member of its class?

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 37, §4:**
> "By isolating what all the tones have in common, we can generate a
> prototypical tone that captures none of the features of any tone in
> particular. When several different horns were analyzed, the resulting
> prototype sounded like a bland, generic horn sound. This result is perhaps not
> musically useful, but if we subtract the prototype matrix from all the
> individual instrument matrices, the prototype becomes the origin of a
> multidimensional coordinate system within which each instrument is located at
> a unique point relative to that origin."

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 37, §4:**
> "Note that a prototypical timbre could have been synthesized from a simple
> average of all the PVA data matrices in the set of timbres, but we found that
> this result had objectionable idiosyncrasies that did not appear in the more
> idealized PC-based prototype."

**Martens 1987, p. 281, §7:**
> "It was hypothesized that using the idealized scores described in the previous
> section for Y* would result in the construction of a matrix of HRTF's that
> contain more robust directional cues than the HRTF's measured for either
> subject. The implicit assumption here is that HRTF behavior should exhibit
> smooth spectral variation along dimensions relevant to directional
> perception."

## Interpolation — mechanism and space

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 37, §4:**
> "The primary source of information for synthesis is in the PCs for deviation
> scores. Once the prototype is generated, it becomes a 'center of gravity' for
> the space within which interpolation takes place. ... Since the interpolation
> space is defined by 3 PCs, there are multiple paths between each of the
> analyzed timbres. We can take the shortest path between two timbres, or follow
> a piecewise linear path that changes values on one dimension at a time."

**Martens 1987, p. 280, §7 — the inverse transform:**
> "If the small proportion of residual variance on the remaining 20 principal
> components is regarded as inconsequential, then the scores on components 5
> through 24 can be set to zero to create a truncated matrix Y* of principal
> component scores. ... A new matrix X* of HRTF band energies can be constructed
> from the truncated matrix Y* of scores through postmultiplying by the inverse
> of the principal components rotation matrix:  X* = Y* U⁻¹"

**Martens 1987, p. 274, abstract:**
> "In each case, the obtained scores could be fit by smooth curves defining
> smooth paths through the low-dimensional principal components space, and
> providing high-quality interpolation for resynthesized spectra."

## How many components, and how chosen

**Sandell & Martens 1992, pp. 35–36, §3:**
> "An exact recovery of the original dataset is guaranteed by using all PCs.
> However, when using only the 3 PCs we found necessary to capture the
> perceptually relevant variance, the result was data reduction greater than a
> factor of 67 in our examples."

**Martens 1987, p. 277, §4:**
> "In order to determine how many principal components should be included in the
> data reduction, the cumulative proportion of variance contained within the
> first several components was examined... Adopting an arbitrary cutoff of 90 %
> selects four components for both subjects. Taken together, the first four
> components account for 91.3 % of the total variance for subject MDL, and
> 91.4 % of the variance for subject DJF."

## Preprocessing — centering, log, critical bands

**Martens 1987, p. 276, §3:**
> "As a precursor to PCA, the estimated critical band energies were
> log-transformed to simulate loudness transduction. Not only is
> log-transformation generally regarded as a more perceptually relevant coding
> than linear coding (Stautner, 1983), but also in direct comparisons, Zahorian
> & Rothenberg (1981) showed that such coding of spectral band amplitude for PCA
> of speech spectra produced superior results when the intelligibility of
> resynthesized speech was used as a criterion."

**Martens 1987, p. 277, §3 — row- vs column-centering, decided empirically:**
> "It should be noted that PCA is typically performed on data matrices that have
> been 'centered by columns,' and that the data matrix currently under analysis
> has been 'centered by rows.' ... As compared with column-centering,
> double-centering (both rows and columns), and no-centering, the best agreement
> between the two subjects' PCA results (MDL and DJF) was obtained by
> row-centering. Coupled with the rationale that overall HRTF level differences
> should be eliminated from the data in order to isolate spectral differences,
> this finding led to the conclusion that row-centering was the best choice of
> the treatments."

**Martens 1987, p. 277, §3:**
> "Though 'row-centering' band energy in this way eliminates some of the
> information present in the raw band energies, it ensures that the PCA to
> follow will examine only spectral variation in HRTF's (in effect, it excludes
> directionally-dependent level differences in HRTF's from the analysis)."

24 critical-band peak frequencies used (Martens 1987, p. 276, Fig. 1): 166, 282,
410, 543, 681, 825, 980, 1155, 1368, 1616, 1909, 2255, 2664, 3146, 3716, 4390,
5185, 6125, 7235, 8545, 10094, 11923, 14083, 16634 Hz.

## PCA describes overall shape, not peaks

**Martens 1987, p. 275, §1:**
> "Similar applications of PCA to vowel spectra have yielded good results that
> can be used for vowel identification (e.g., Plomp, Pols, & van de Geer, 1967).
> The principal components obtained in such studies are regarded as 'spectral
> shape factors,' in contrast with the more conventional formant descriptions of
> speech spectra. Zahorian & Rothenberg (1981) point out that such PCA results
> best describe the overall shape of the speech spectra, and place less emphasis
> on the major spectral peaks."

**Martens 1987, p. 277, §4:**
> "Note that principal component 1 for both subjects weights low-frequency bands
> positively and high-frequency bands negatively. This suggests that the
> principal source of variation in centered horizontal plane HRTF's is a
> shifting of energy to and from high-frequency bands as azimuth angle varies."

## Preconditions and limits

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 36, §3:**
> "As a rule, PCA is an efficient data reduction method only when the input
> variables are correlated."

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 36, §3:**
> "Since spectral properties are more likely to be invariant across multiple
> durations and articulations of an instrument than temporal properties, a
> single spectral PCA may offer more insight into an instrument's general
> timbral identity."

**Freed & Martens 1986, p. 395, §2.3.1 — three classes of redundancy:**
> "1. Tautological redundancy. The parameters are correlated because they
> measure the same thing.
> 2. Ecological redundancy. The parameters are always correlated in natural
> sounds, but artificial stimuli can be synthesized in which the parameters vary
> independently. These artificial stimuli are likely to produce unusual
> percepts.
> 3. Identifying redundancy. The correlation between the parameters is an
> identifying characteristic of the class of timbres under investigation."

**Freed & Martens 1986, p. 396:**
> "When performing data reduction by eliminating redundancy, it is important to
> consider these distinctions. For example, if the redundancy is an identifying
> feature of some class of timbres, then the data reduction scheme is not
> applicable outside that class of timbres, whereas if the redundancy is
> tautological, the data reduction may be applied universally."

**Freed & Martens 1986, p. 403, §3.5 — extrapolation beyond the fitted set:**
> "If, however, the stimulus set upon which the regression coefficients are
> based did not cover the range of sounds for which predictions are desired, it
> is possible that extrapolation to new stimuli would incur large errors."

**Freed & Martens 1986, p. 395, §2.3.1 — what a global description loses:**
> "A global description is incomplete in the sense that it is impossible to
> exactly resynthesize the original sound from the description. However, if the
> global parameters successfully capture the most important acoustical
> properties of the sound, the loss of local detail should be perceptually
> unimportant."

**Sandell & Martens 1992, p. 37, §5 — why parametric approximations are rejected
as PCA input:**
> "We note that the data reduction provided by VDTP downsampling is not nearly
> as great as that obtained by fitting line-segment approximations to each
> amplitude envelope (see Grey, 1975), but such approximations are not suitable
> for PCA, and do not evince many of the desireable features of VDTP
> downsampling."

## Cross-subject component matching breaks down after PC2

**Martens 1987, pp. 277–278, §4:**
> "While a comparison of the rotation weights defining the first principal
> component for each subject reveals a correlation coefficient of r = 0.96, the
> second principal components for the two subjects correlate at r = 0.88. It is
> clear that components 1 and 2 match across subject without confusion, but
> components 3 and 4 are less well matched between the two subjects. Although
> components 3 and 4 are strictly orthogonal for a single subject, they are
> fairly well correlated between subjects."

## Papers with no bearing

**Martens 1985 (PALETTE)** — contains no PCA of spectra, no prototype or mean
construction, and no interpolation in a component space. Its analyses are
unidimensional psychophysical curve-fitting, MDS, additive-tree fitting and
hierarchical clustering on listener similarity judgments.

**Freed & Martens 1986** — its PCA is over 8 scalar acoustical parameters × 21
stimuli, not over spectra. No passage on interpolation space.
